Perhaps you're already aware of this, but if not, too many WordPress plugins can slow your website down, or worse, it can take your site down altogether.

Having specialized in WordPress since 2010 I've seen this happen countless times, and more often than not the business owner is the last one to know.

Imagine getting a call from a potential client who's the first to inform you that your website is down, or worse directing visitors to nefarious websites. It isn't exactly good for business.

To that end, cutting down on plugins is an essential step in keeping your WordPress installation lean and clean, and reduces the risk of it breaking or getting hacked.

That being said, I'm here to share some pointers on selecting the best ones while keeping your site zippy and secure.

You'll get the the full scoop on selecting compatible plugins, **timely updates**, and ditching the dead weight.

### Key Takeaways

- Too many plugins make sites **slow and easy for hackers**. Keep them few.
- Update your plugins often to **stop security problems**.
- Only use necessary plugins that align with the goals of your site.
- Remove plugins you don't use to **help your website work better**.
- Pick plugins that do many things at once from **trusted makers**.

## The Problems with Too Many WordPress Plugins

![](./images/too-many-wordpress-plugins-bad-for-business/the-problems-with-too-many-wordpress-plugins-330286728.jpg)

Having too many WordPress plugins can cause [security vulnerabilities](/blog/why-your-wordpress-emails-are-flagged-as-spam-how-to-fix-it/), site crashes, and [slow page speeds](/blog/wordpress-slow-how-to-fix/).

It's essential to keep the number of plugins to a minimum for a smooth and secure website experience.

### Security Vulnerabilities

![](./images/too-many-wordpress-plugins-bad-for-business/security-vulnerabilities-330286201.jpg)

Don't get me wrong: much like iPhone or Android apps, plugins can make a WordPress site do almost anything you want.

But, they also open doors to hackers. I've seen this firsthand while helping businesses with their WordPress sites.

Hackers look for **outdated plugins** because they're easy targets. These old plugins might have **security holes** that haven't been fixed yet.

Updating plugins is key to keeping your site safe. It's like shutting the door on hackers. Yet, some folks forget or put off updates.

This makes their sites a risk for attacks like **cross-site scripting** where bad code gets into your website without you knowing.

By choosing plugins carefully and keeping them up to date, we cut down this risk big time.

### Site Crashes and Conflicts

![](./images/too-many-wordpress-plugins-bad-for-business/site-crashes-and-conflicts-330286711.jpg)

I learned a tough lesson once. A client had about 30 plugins on their site. Many were **out of date** or did not work well together.

The site broke so badly, we had to start over and build it again. This shows how too many plugins can cause big problems.

Plugins that do not get along can also make your site act weird. It's like trying to cook with too many chefs in the kitchen; things get messy fast.

I saw this happen with a plugin called WP Bakery. When I deleted it, it left behind code that broke the entire layout and required a redesign.

To avoid these issues, always check if plugins play nice before adding them to your site. Ideally you should be doing so inside a staging environment and not on your live site.

### Slow Page Speed & Poor Performance

![](./images/too-many-wordpress-plugins-bad-for-business/slow-page-speed-and-poor-performance-330286274.jpg)

To make matters worse, too many WordPress plugins can slow a site down considerably, and can even break your site altogether. I've seen it happen too often.

You need your site to be fast for both visitors and search engines alike. Using tools like [Google's Pagespeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) will show exactly what's slowing your site down.

My recommendation is that you keep installed plugins under 10 to avoid **slow speeds** and **bad performance**. Every few months, I check each plugin's job on my site health.

If one doesn't help much, I remove it. This keeps my client's websites running smooth and quick.

### Only Install Essential Plugins

![](./images/too-many-wordpress-plugins-bad-for-business/only-install-essential-plugins-330286179.jpg)

Having had my hands in WordPress almost every day since 2010, I've seen first hand how too many WordPress plugins can be bad for business

For **growth-focused small business owners**, it's crucial to choose only the necessary ones to avoid potential issues.

Security and performance plugins are essential to [managing WordPress effectively](/blog/wordpress-management-strategies-for-marketers/) and keeping your WordPress installation on a solid foundation.

By focusing on essential plugins like these, you **streamline your WordPress setup** for optimal efficiency.

### Regularly Update and Remove Unused Plugins

![](./images/too-many-wordpress-plugins-bad-for-business/regularly-update-and-remove-unused-plugins-330286708.jpg)

Regularly updating and removing unused plugins is essential for [a healthy website](/blog/wordpress-help/). Outdated plugins can create security vulnerabilities, making the site susceptible to malicious attacks.

By keeping these tools up-to-date with bug fixes and security patches, I ensure that my website remains secure and protected from potential threats.

In addition, regularly evaluating and removing unused plugins helps maintain the site's performance. Unnecessary plugins can slow down **page loading speed** and lead to **conflicts among different components**.

By trimming down the number of active plugins to only essential ones, I optimize my **website's efficiency** while reducing the risk of crashes or malfunctions.

### Choose Reputable and Multi-Functional Plugins

![](./images/too-many-wordpress-plugins-bad-for-business/choose-reputable-and-multi-functional-plugins-330286943.jpg)

Now, let's talk about choosing reputable and multi-functional plugins. It's crucial to select plugins from **reputable sources** to avoid security issues and ensure good performance.

Seek plugins with **multiple features** so you can minimize the number of plugins on your site while maximizing their functions.

This helps maintain a lean and efficient website, which is important for [small businesses aiming for growth](/blog/reclaim-lost-time-get-kick-ass-marketing-results/).

When selecting plugins, always check the reviews and ratings to ensure they meet your needs without causing problems later on.

Choose versatile tools like Uncanny Automator that can substitute several single-purpose plugins at once, saving you time and effort in managing them.

Keep in mind, fewer well-chosen plugins mean less risk of conflicts or vulnerabilities – a win-win situation for any growth-focused business owner!
